\section*{Basic Probability}
Probability of an event $A$ occuring is written as $P(A)$, where:
\[
0 \leq P(A) \leq 1
\]
\section*{Conditional Probability}
The conditional probability of event $A$ given $B$ is:
\[
P(A \mid B)
\]
This is read as \textit{`` The probability of $A$ given $B$."}
\section*{Intersection and Union}
The probability of both event $A$ and $B$ occuring simultaneously is:
\[
P(A \cap B) 
\]
The probability of either event $A$ or $B$ occuring is:
\[
P(A \cup B)
\]
\section*{Compliment}
The compliment of an event $A$ is the probability that $A$ does not occur:
\[
p(A^c)
\]
This follows the fundamental rule:
\[
P(A) + P(A^c) = 1
\]
\section*{Expectation}
Expectation of a random variable $X$ represents its long run average value: 
\[
\mathbb{E}[x]
\]
The conditional expectation of $X$ given $Y$ is:
\[
\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]
\]
\section*{Variance and Covariance}
Variance of a random variable $X$ measures how much it spreads around its mean:
\[
\text{Var}(X)
\]
Covariance of two random variable $X$ and $Y$ measures how much they vary together.
\[
\text{Cov}(X, Y)
\]
These two quantities are connected by the identity:
\[
\text{Cov}(X, X) = \text{Var}(X)
\]
\section*{Probability Distributions}
A random variable $X$ follows a normal distribution with mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$:
\[
X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)
\]
Other common distribution is data science:
\[
X \sim \text{Bernoulli}(p)
\]
\[
X \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p)
\]
\[
X \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda)
\]
